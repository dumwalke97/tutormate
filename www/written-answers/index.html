How a written answer is judged

After the explanation is written, the page highlights the student's answer as a match when the text lines up with the correct answer. Capitalization and extra spaces are ignored. A sentence that is right in substance but worded differently can fail that highlight. The explanation is the part to read in that case. It is not a rubric score from your teacher, and it is not a claim that the wording match is a grade.

Numbers on the same page are handled separately. Two numbers within 0.01 count as a match, which is the rule described on the algebra page. Parts labeled like "a)" and "b)", or "1." and "2.", are compared one part at a time when both the student's answer and the correct answer use those labels. You do not choose a subject first. History, vocabulary, and science short answers go through the same check as a math page. More of the workflow is on homework help.

How to check the page

  1. Answer in your own handwriting or typing. The check needs a completed attempt on the page.
  2. Photograph the whole answer. A cropped line is easier to misread. Handwriting is supported when the photo is clear and lit.
  3. Read the explanation beside the highlight. A green highlight means the text matched. It does not mean every possible correct wording would match.
  4. Practice the terms you missed. The quiz generator can turn the same notes into four-choice questions. A biology example of that shape is on the photosynthesis page.

What this is not

It is not an essay grader, and it does not return a rubric score for a multi-paragraph paper. It is built for the question-by-question page: a definition, a vocabulary item, a short answer. It also does not replace a solver when you have not started a math problem. That difference is spelled out in TutorMate versus Photomath.

Common questions

Can TutorMate check answers that are not math?

Yes. The checker reads the page and writes down the answer it sees, the correct answer, and an explanation. That includes definitions, vocabulary, and short history or science answers. The first 10 checks are free, then $4.95 per month or $49 per year.

What if my sentence is right but worded differently?

A written answer is highlighted as a match when the text lines up with the correct answer, ignoring capitalization and extra spaces. A correct idea in different words may not get that highlight. Read the explanation, which is where the reasoning is written out. Numbers are different: they count as a match within 0.01.

Can it check a page that mixes sentences and calculations?

Yes. One uploaded page can contain both. Numeric items use the number comparison. Sentences use the text comparison. You do not pick a subject mode before you upload.
