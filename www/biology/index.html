A photosynthesis quiz from your own notes

TutorMate builds a multiple-choice biology quiz from a topic you type, notes you paste, or a page you photograph. The quiz box on the app uses "10th Grade Biology Photosynthesis" as one of its two example topics. Your first 10 quizzes are free.

Why the quiz is built from your material

A general biology bank will ask about every unit. Your test usually will not. If this week is photosynthesis, a quiz made from your worksheet or from that topic stays on chlorophyll, the chloroplast, and the two stages of the reactions. Re-reading the notes makes the page look familiar. Answering a question forces you to retrieve the fact, which is the practice that transfers to a test. The quiz generator is the full description of the four ways to start.

What the quiz button actually makes

Questions are multiple choice, with exactly four options, and the marked answer is one of those four strings. On a new quiz you choose 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, or 30 questions. If you are quizzing files you already saved in a folder, the count can go up to 100. You can start from a typed topic, pasted notes, a photo, a PDF, or a Word document. There is no separate biology mode and no promise that every generated question will be the one above. The model writes new questions from the material you give it, so a different set of notes produces a different quiz.

You can also photograph a finished biology worksheet and check the short answers, the same way you would check any other completed page. That path is described on homework help and written answers. Numeric work, if the sheet has calculations, is covered with the algebra check.

How to make the quiz

  1. Use the material you will be tested on. A typed topic works when you have nothing else. Your own notes are closer to the test.
  2. Pick a count you will finish. Ten questions is the default on a new quiz. Four choices each.
  3. Miss a question, then find it in the notes. The point is to see the gap while the worksheet is still around.
  4. Save it. Folders sync between the browser and the iPhone app, so the retake does not have to happen at the same desk.
